Coastal legislators are pressing for a dedicated ferry maintenance fund after a winter of cancelled sailings and emergency dry-dock visits that scrambled commutes.

The proposal would ring-fence a slice of transportation revenue rather than force ferries to compete annually with highway projects. Inland members call the structure inflexible.

> Union marine engineers testified that overtime on aging systems is itself a risk.

Regional media coverage split along familiar lines. Morning radio framed the delay as chaos; policy newsletters framed it as normal legislating under pressure.

Legal counsel reminded the body that findings of fact in the staff report will be read closely if litigation follows. That warning slowed the rhetoric more effectively than the gavel.

The next hearing will tell whether the revised map is a genuine compromise or a device to run out the calendar. Housing advocates say they will measure acres and months, not adjectives.

Union marine engineers testified that overtime on aging systems is itself a risk. “We can keep them running,” said engineer chief Paul Okonkwo. “We cannot keep pretending running is free.”

The governor’s office floated a compromise: a two-year surge appropriation with audit requirements, postponing structural change. Coastal leaders called that a patch.

A committee vote is expected before the recess. Port Meridian’s delegation is unified; the swing votes sit in river districts with their own bridge backlog.

Lobbyists flooded the anteroom with one-pagers that recycled last year’s arguments in new fonts. Staffers sorted them into three piles: housing supply, neighborhood character, and process complaints.

Outside chambers, organizers collected emails for a rapid-response list. Inside, the parliamentarian struggled to keep amendments within scope as members tested the edges of the draft.

People closest to the story keep returning to a practical question: what changes on Monday morning. Titles and press notes matter less than schedules, budgets, and who answers the phone.

For The Harbor Ledger’s readers, the significance sits in the cumulative effect. One announcement rarely remakes the waterfront; a season of them can. This piece will be updated as primary documents and on-the-record responses arrive.
