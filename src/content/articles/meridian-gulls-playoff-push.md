The Meridian Gulls sit two points clear of the wild-card line with five matches left, a position almost nobody in this city predicted after a dreary autumn that produced two wins in eleven and a supporters' meeting that ran past midnight.

The run that changed it started on 2 March. Since then the Gulls have taken 26 points from a possible 36, conceding eight goals across those twelve matches. The problem is that they have done it with, essentially, thirteen players. Coach Imelda Shore has named the same back four in fifteen consecutive league fixtures. Her two starting central midfielders, Rasmus Teigen and Ondine Falk, are now both out — Teigen with a grade-two hamstring tear that has him listed at four to six weeks, Falk with a foot injury the club has described only as “under assessment,” which in Shore's vocabulary is not encouraging.

Friday's training session at the Longspur Road ground featured three academy call-ups taking first-team repetitions in the midfield shape, watched from the touchline by roughly sixty supporters who are allowed in on open sessions and who applauded a nineteen-year-old, Bram Ostrowe, for a routine sideways pass.

> Supporters remain loud.

Shore refused to talk about the table, as she has refused all season, with a consistency that has moved from mildly irritating to genuinely impressive. “We talk about the next ninety minutes and the next recovery day,” she said. Asked directly whether the club had begun planning for a first-round playoff road trip, she said that was a question for people with spreadsheets. Club staff have, in fact, held provisional hotel space in two cities since June.

## The load problem

The medical and performance staff have reshaped the training week around the squad's actual capacity rather than its intended one. Full-contact sessions have been cut from roughly 40 minutes to 22. Recovery days have gone from one to two in weeks with a midweek fixture. GPS load data, a curiosity for supporters two seasons ago, now quietly determines who starts friendlies and who spends Tuesday on a bike.

“We are managing a squad of thirteen through a schedule built for twenty-two,” said head of performance Ingrid Vasari. “There is no clever solution to that. There is only choosing which risk you take. Every minute I give Teigen back early is a minute I am borrowing from August.”

Local radio has turned every visible limp into a fifteen-minute segment, which amuses the players and irritates the medical department. “Speculation is free,” one physiotherapist said, watching a caller's theory about Falk scroll across a studio feed. “Ligaments are not.”

The counter-argument inside the club is not about caution but about the academy. Youth director Marcus Ilenko has argued, at times sharply, that the thin bench is an opportunity being wasted by conservatism. He points out that four academy graduates have made a combined 71 minutes of league appearances this season.

“Everyone says depth is built in March and cannot be burned in April,” Ilenko said. “Fine. But depth is also not built by sitting a nineteen-year-old on a bench for a season so he can be safe. At some point somebody has to actually play. We have five matches where the alternative is a thirty-four-year-old at ninety percent.”

Shore has not conceded the point publicly. She gave Ostrowe eleven minutes in the last match, and parents in the academy stand counted every one of them.

## The city's logistics problem

Ticket demand has already forced a temporary release of upper-deck sections in the north stand that have been curtained off since 2019. Season-ticket renewals for next year opened three weeks early and passed 9,400 in nine days, against a stadium capacity of 14,800.

City transit planners are modeling playoff crowds now rather than in October. A home playoff match would require roughly $38,000 in overtime for extra late bus service on the 4, 11, and 22 routes, plus additional harbor-front stewarding. The transit authority has not committed. Its position, stated at a committee meeting last week, is that it will not pay overtime for a fixture that has not been scheduled.

“Empty sidewalks after a loss cost a different kind of goodwill,” said transit planner Odile Sarraf, who has run the numbers three times. “But I cannot buy driver hours with goodwill. I need a date.”

The club's own front office has a narrower version of the same problem. Any home playoff match must be confirmed to the league fourteen days out, and the stadium's temporary seating contractor requires ten days' notice. That leaves a four-day window in which the Gulls must commit to costs for a fixture they may not host. Commercial director Yusuf Almeida put the exposure at about $90,000. “We will spend it,” he said. “I would just prefer to spend it in a week when I know the answer.”

The east stand's new tifo collective, which formed in January and has about ninety members, debuted a harbor-crane design last weekend that briefly stopped play while stewards cleared streamers from the corner flag. The referee's report noted a two-minute delay. The collective has apologized and announced that the next display will use fewer streamers and more fabric.

Whatever the table says on the last day, the club's near-term identity is fixed: a thin roster, a loud ground, and a coaching staff that will not narrate the standings out loud.

The Gulls play at Ardenhall on Saturday. Teigen has been told he will not travel. He asked anyway, twice, and was told again.
