The Meridian Gulls finished the five-match run that began in mid-July with two wins, one draw and two losses, took seven points from a possible fifteen, and clinched the second wild-card place on Saturday by a single point over Bellhaven with a goal in the 88th minute from a player who was in the academy in March.

Ronen Tiff is nineteen. He came on in the 71st, missed a header that the east stand is still describing charitably, and then arrived at the back post off a short corner that Coach Imelda Shore had drawn on a whiteboard on Thursday and, by two players’ accounts, had not run once in training because there was not a full eleven available to run it against.

“We walked it,” midfielder Casimir Bell said. “Walked it, at half speed, on Friday, with three of the staff standing in as defenders. One of them is fifty-four.”

## The cost of the run

Shore’s rotation held, barely. The two starting midfielders whose injuries defined the July reporting — Aurelio Kest and Sanne Doorn — returned for a combined 214 minutes across the five matches, none of them consecutive, both on the strict load ceilings the medical staff imposed in June.

Four academy players appeared on a matchday sheet during the stretch. Two, Tiff and defender Marisol Ige, played meaningful minutes. Ige, eighteen, has now started three consecutive league matches at right back and leads the squad in duels won per ninety since July 25.

The squad played the five matches with an average of 15.8 available senior outfield players against a league average of 21.4, according to the club’s own medical returns. Head of performance Yusra Delacroix said the staff cut full-contact training volume by 38 percent over the stretch and moved two sessions entirely into the pool.

“You can do that for five weeks,” Delacroix said. “You cannot do it for a playoff run and a preseason and then a next season. Something at the end of that sentence breaks, and it is usually a hamstring belonging to whoever was cheapest to overuse.”

> A club that survives on nineteen-year-olds in August is either building something or spending something, and you rarely know which one until the following spring.

## The pitch by the channel

The academy ground that opened in March hosted its first competitive fixtures in the same stretch, which the club has treated as a footnote and which academy director Sofia Maren does not.

Three under-18 matches and one reserve fixture were played on the channel surface between August 1 and August 15. The 9:30 p.m. floodlight stop negotiated with neighbors held; the reserve match on August 8 kicked at 6:00 specifically to clear it, and finished with eleven minutes of daylight to spare.

Maren said drainage performed through the one heavy evening in the stretch, which was the point of building it. The complaint log from the path walkers holds four entries since March, three of them about parking on Culver Lane and one about a ball.

The academy’s problem is now the opposite of the one it had in March. Maren has two of her best players — Tiff and Ige — training with the first team and unavailable to the age-group sides that the pitch was built to develop. The under-18s lost 4-0 on August 12 with both out.

“I asked for a pitch so children would stop driving ninety minutes to train,” she said. “I did not ask for a conveyor belt. Those are different buildings and we are running the first one as if it were the second.”

## The argument the front office is having

Sporting director Aleš Hovnik has to decide something in the next eleven days, and both answers are defensible.

The club has roughly $2.1 million of headroom before it hits its self-imposed wage ceiling, and the window closes September 1. Shore wants a senior central midfielder — an experienced one, on a short deal, specifically to protect Kest and Doorn through the playoffs.

Hovnik has not committed. His argument, made at some length to season-ticket holders at a Tuesday forum where he could have simply said nothing, is that the club’s wage bill has grown 31 percent in two seasons on the back of one good March, that the playoff revenue is a single-elimination coin flip and cannot be budgeted, and that the academy investment only pays back if the pathway stays open at the top.

“If I sign a thirty-three-year-old on a six-month deal, I have told Ronen Tiff and Marisol Ige exactly where they stand,” he said. “They are nineteen and eighteen. They will hear it correctly.”

The counter came from the floor, from a supporter named Wilhelmina Oke who has held the same two seats in the east stand since 2003 and who was not interested in the philosophy.

“You are describing a plan for 2029 to people who watched us get relegated in 2018,” she said. “Ligaments do not care about your pathway. If Doorn goes in the first leg and we go out and the crowd falls back to nine thousand next season, there is no academy money, because there is no money. Sign the boring midfielder.”

Hovnik said afterward he thought that was the strongest version of the case against him and that he had not answered it well.

## Practical matters

The first-round tie is away, September 6, at a venue the club has not been to since 2019. City transit staff have confirmed that the late-bus overtime modeled in July will only be needed if the Gulls host a second leg, which requires them to win the first.

Ticketing has released 1,400 away allocation and expects to clear it. The tifo collective, whose harbor-crane design briefly stopped play in July, has told the club it is working on something for the away end and has declined to say what, which the safety officer has noted in writing.

Shore, asked on Saturday whether clinching changed how she would talk about the season, said no, and then broke her own rule about the standings for one sentence.

“We are one of eight,” she said. “For eleven months I have told these players to look at the next ninety minutes. On Monday I am going to show them the bracket, once, for about four minutes, and then we are putting it away.”
