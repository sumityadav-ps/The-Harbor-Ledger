A new coastal geology survey maps segments of outer-shore bluff retreating faster than the long-term average, with implications for setback policies and insurance conversations across roughly nine miles of Port Meridian's exposed western edge.

The survey, produced over twenty months by the Meridian Coastal Processes Group with county cost-sharing, combines lidar flown in three passes, historical oblique photographs dating to 1938, and borehole data from fourteen locations between Cormorant Point and the Vessey Road turnaround. Its authors refuse single-number predictions for individual parcels, offering scenario bands instead.

The headline finding is uneven. Along the 2.4-mile stretch north of the old ferry landing, where the bluff face is glacial till capped with loose sand, the study's central estimate for retreat is 1.1 to 1.9 feet per year, against a hundred-year average closer to 0.7. South of the landing, where a shelf of cemented sandstone sits at the toe, the rate barely moved. Two segments appear to have gained material, most likely from slumping upslope rather than any real accretion.

> Some homeowners heard "faster" and called for armored walls.

## Why the numbers come in bands

Dr. Owen Marchetti, the group's lead geomorphologist, spent much of a January public meeting at the Vessey Road fire hall explaining why he would not tell any single household when its lawn would end.

"A bluff does not retreat the way a candle burns," he said. "It sits for eleven years and then loses fourteen feet in a February weekend. If I give you an annual average and you build to it, I have lied to you politely."

The team's scenario bands reflect three variables that behave independently: the frequency of storms arriving on a high spring tide, the amount of groundwater the bluff carries after a wet autumn, and whether the beach in front of a given segment holds enough sand that winter to absorb wave energy. In the survey's high-end scenario, which assumes two saturated winters in a decade, eleven parcels along Gannet Row lose their setback margin before 2050. In the low-end scenario, none do.

Fieldwork was less elegant than the maps suggest. Technicians on the project logged 63 site visits and lost three ground-truth sensors to gulls, salt corrosion, and one instance of a firmware update that silently reset a logging interval. Roughly a fifth of the survey's budget went to maintenance and repeat visits rather than analysis.

## Seawalls, and the argument against them

The clearest disagreement in the room was about armoring. Seven households along the fastest-retreating segment have discussed a shared revetment, and one homeowner, Beatrice Lunn, has already obtained an engineer's estimate of $410,000 for a 320-foot section.

Geologists at the meeting explained how armoring can shift erosion to neighbors. A hard structure reflects wave energy rather than absorbing it, scouring sand from the beach in front of the wall and accelerating loss at the unarmored properties on either flank — a process the survey documents at a small privately built bulkhead near Cormorant Point, where the two adjacent lots have retreated an estimated 40 percent faster than the segment average since 2009.

The room was not uniformly convinced. Lunn, who has lived on the bluff since 1997 and watched a neighbor's outbuilding go over in 2019, put the counter-argument bluntly and, several attendees conceded afterward, fairly.

"I am told my wall harms the beach. Fine. Nobody has offered to buy my house," she said. "You are asking me to accept a loss on behalf of a coastline that does not send anyone a mortgage statement. Tell me who pays, and then tell me about sediment budgets."

County planning director Sanjay Oduya, who attended but did not present, acknowledged afterward that the county has no answer yet. Its acquisition fund holds $1.6 million, enough for perhaps two purchases at current bluff-lot valuations, and the council has not authorized a standing buyout program. Buyouts remain politically difficult and fiscally limited.

County planning will use the maps in a setback update expected next year. Staff have signaled that the update will likely replace the current uniform 50-foot line with a variable setback keyed to segment, a change that would tighten requirements on about 140 parcels and loosen them on roughly 60.

## Publishing carefully, on purpose

Public communication remains a deliberate practice for the group. Figures are released with uncertainty bands, and press officers have stopped promising single-number headlines the data cannot support — a policy adopted after a 2023 regional broadcast compressed an earlier study's range into a single alarming figure that the team spent four months walking back.

A plain-language summary is available at the Harbor Branch and Vessey Road libraries; the full technical volume, 214 pages with appendices, is for those who enjoy uncertainty quantified carefully.

Replication plans are already in motion with a partner lab inland, which will re-run the lidar differencing on the same raw returns using independent software. Independent confirmation is the price of any municipal interest in the work, researchers said, and the group budgeted for it before publishing.

Students on the project are writing methods sections that double as training manuals for the next cohort. Continuity is a research output when grants turn over every two years, and Marchetti said he expects to lose two of his four field techs to graduation before the next survey cycle begins.

The survey's own record of prediction is short and mixed. In 2021 the group forecast a slump at station 14 within a decade; the slope failed the following March, eight years early. It also flagged station 6 as unstable, and station 6 has not moved measurably since.

At the end of the fire hall meeting, a man in his seventies asked whether the maps could tell him if the house was worth repainting. Marchetti said the honest answer was probably yes, and that he would not have said the same about a new foundation. The man wrote something on the back of the plain-language summary, folded it into his coat, and walked out into a night in which the surf, four hundred yards off, was doing what it does at a rate nobody in the room could agree on.
