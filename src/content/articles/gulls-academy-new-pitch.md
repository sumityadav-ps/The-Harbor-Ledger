The Meridian Gulls Academy opened a full-size training pitch on reclaimed land by the channel, giving youth teams a dedicated surface with lights and modern drainage.

The site is a former asphalt yard behind the grain elevators, four hectares that the port authority stopped using for container staging in 2019. Turning it into a pitch required stripping 40 centimeters of contaminated fill, laying a sand-and-gravel drainage raft, and installing 3,100 meters of perforated lateral pipe. Grounds manager Teodor Bask, who spent eleven years maintaining school fields on the north side, says the raft is the part he cares about. "Everyone photographs the lights," he said. "The lights do not stop you losing eight weekends to standing water."

Funding mixed club capital, a municipal youth-sports grant, and naming rights from a regional credit union. Community hours are built into the lease: public pickup is free two evenings a week.

> Academy director Sofia Maren said the pitch is as much about reducing travel as producing pros.

"Parents were logging coastal highway hours for ordinary training," she said.

## The arithmetic of the drive

Maren has a spreadsheet she has now shown to three funders and one skeptical council committee. Before the build, the academy's nine age groups trained across five borrowed venues, two of them past the ridge tunnel. An under-14 player living in the Basin district averaged 71 minutes of round-trip travel per session, three sessions a week. Multiplied across a 34-week season, that is roughly 120 hours in a car.

The attrition data underneath it is blunter. Of the 218 players who left the academy over the past four seasons, exit forms cited travel or scheduling as the primary reason in 96 cases — more than injury, cost, and loss of interest combined. "We were not losing children who stopped loving football," Maren said. "We were losing children whose families ran out of Tuesdays."

The total build came to 3.4 million, of which the club put up 1.6 million from reserves and a mortgage against its clubhouse. The municipal youth-sports grant contributed 900,000 across two tranches, the second contingent on the club hitting its community-hours obligation for a full year. Naming rights from Channelside Credit Union cover 640,000 over eight years. The remainder came from a bond drive that sold 2,100 named paving bricks along the entry path.

## Not everyone signed the card

Councillor Ilse Roth, who represents the ridge wards, voted against the second grant tranche and has not changed her position. Her objection is not the pitch. It is that the youth-sports fund is capped at 1.1 million a year and a single applicant took 82 percent of one cycle.

"I have twelve schools with netball courts that flood," Roth said. "I am asked to celebrate a facility that serves one club, on the far side of the channel, that already had the strongest balance sheet of any applicant. Concentration is a choice. We made it."

Maren does not dispute the arithmetic and says the community-hours clause was written partly in response to Roth. Two evenings a week the gates open with no booking and no fee; the club counted 1,340 individual visits in the first five weeks, tracked by a volunteer with a clicker at the north gate. Roth has said she will look at those numbers again in November and that a good year would change her vote, not her argument.

Neighbors negotiated a hard stop on floodlights at 9:30 p.m. and a landscape buffer along the path. Early reaction from walkers has been cautiously positive. The buffer — 140 alder and sea buckthorn, planted at two meters — was the club's concession after a lighting study showed spill onto the towpath at levels the residents' association called "a second moon."

The academy pathway is under new scrutiny as well. Parents want minutes; coaches want patience. The club's message is that depth built in March cannot be burned in a single April sprint. Training staff have shortened full-contact sessions and lengthened recovery protocols. GPS load data, once a curiosity for fans, now quietly dictates who starts midweek friendlies.

Local radio has turned every bruise into a segment, which amuses players and irritates the medical team. "Speculation is free," one physiotherapist said. "Ligaments are not."

City transit planners are already modeling crowds for the summer friendlies. The 14 bus terminates at the elevators and runs hourly after seven; a proposal to add two late services on Wednesday and Saturday would cost the transit authority about 47,000 a year, and no one has yet said who pays it.

First competitive matches on the surface are scheduled for late summer friendlies.

Bask has one worry he raises unprompted. The channel wind comes across the reclaimed ground at an angle nothing blocks, and in the first fortnight it dried the new sward faster than the irrigation heads could keep up along the eastern touchline. He has taken to walking that line at six in the morning with a soil probe, pushing it in every ten paces, reading the depth mark against his thumbnail. On the day the under-16s played their first session under lights, he was still out there at kickoff, at the far corner, not watching.
