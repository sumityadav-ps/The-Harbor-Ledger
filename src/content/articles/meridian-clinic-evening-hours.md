Meridian Community Clinic will keep primary care open until 8 p.m. on weekdays starting next month, a pilot aimed at patients who lose wages when they leave work for daytime appointments.

The extension required staggered shifts rather than simple overtime. Two nurse practitioners and a rotating physician cover the later block, with lab draw services extended three nights a week.

> Patients at a preview forum asked about childcare and pharmacy hours.

Community health workers are being looped into reminder calls for patients with transportation barriers. Early pilots suggest that a human voice outperforms app pings for no-show reduction in this zip code cluster.

Data sharing between the clinic, pharmacy, and hospital remains partial. Different record systems still force faxing for some referrals, a fact staff mention with practiced irritation.

Public health partners see the pilot as a test of whether access expansions can stick without burning out the same small set of providers. Staggered shifts help; hiring pipelines matter more.

Patients at a preview forum asked about childcare and pharmacy hours. The clinic partnered with a nearby independent pharmacy to match the new schedule on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

Funding blends a state access grant and reallocated vacancy savings. CEO Dr. Naomi Wells warned that if no-show rates spike, the pilot could shrink. Reminder systems are being rewritten in plain language and multiple tongues spoken in the basin.

Early appointment slots released to existing patients first; new patient openings appear in week three.

If metrics hold through winter respiratory season, administrators say they will take a multi-year staffing plan to the board rather than another one-off grant request.

Clinicians caution that extended hours only help if follow-up capacity exists. A late appointment that cannot book labs or imaging simply shifts frustration to another queue.

People closest to the story keep returning to a practical question: what changes on Monday morning. Titles and press notes matter less than schedules, budgets, and who answers the phone.
